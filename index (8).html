Four Branches of Modern Slasher History

How independent collaborators, exploitation distributors, and breakout hits grew into the Crystal Lake, Elm Street, Haddonfield, and Texas Chain Saw families.

Friday the 13th Nightmare on Elm Street Halloween Texas Chain Saw Massacre

Friday the 13th

Defining traits: summer camp, Jason, the hockey mask, and a franchise built from a fast, inexpensive horror formula.

  1. 1971Together

    Sean S. Cunningham directs and produces; Wes Craven synchronizes dailies and helps edit. Hallmark buys the completed film, and its success earns them another assignment.

  2. 1972The Last House on the Left

    Craven writes and directs; Cunningham produces. Roughly 20-year-old Steve Miner joins as production assistant and assistant editor, beginning a long mentorship with the two filmmakers.

  3. 1978Here Come the Tigers + Manny's Orphans

    Cunningham wants to move away from pornography and horror. Phil Scuderi's Georgetown Productions funds two family films modeled on The Bad News Bears.

    The productions assemble the future Friday the 13th team: Steve Miner, writer Victor Miller, cinematographer Barry Abrams, composer Harry Manfredini, producer Susan Cunningham, and young actor Ari Lehman.

  4. 1978-79Halloween succeeds

    Victor Miller recalled Cunningham's pitch: “Halloween is making a lot of money - let's rip it off.” They move the isolation-and-stalking model to a summer camp.

  5. 1980Friday the 13th

    Georgetown Productions finances the film. Cunningham directs; Miller writes; Miner serves as associate producer, assistant director, and unit production manager; Abrams shoots; Manfredini scores.

    Ari Lehman plays young Jason in the final shock, pulling survivor Alice Hardy into Crystal Lake.

  6. 1981Friday the 13th Part 2

    Steve Miner's feature-directing debut. Jason becomes the principal killer, initially hiding beneath a burlap sack.

  7. 1982Friday the 13th Part III

    Miner directs again. Jason acquires the hockey mask that becomes the franchise's defining image.

A Nightmare on Elm Street

Defining traits: urban legend, dream logic, Freddy's supernatural powers, and Wes Craven's complex directorial voice.

  1. 1972Craven's branch breaks away

    After writing and directing The Last House on the Left for producer Sean S. Cunningham, Wes Craven follows his own directorial path toward Elm Street. The earlier Cunningham/Craven collaborations are shown in the Friday the 13th line.

  2. 1975-82Craven develops his own voice

    The Fireworks Woman (1975) - The Hills Have Eyes (1977) - Deadly Blessing (1981) - Swamp Thing (1982).

  3. 1984A Nightmare on Elm Street

    Craven writes and directs, creating Freddy Krueger and launching the Elm Street franchise.

Halloween

Defining traits: Michael Myers, the holiday theme, Carpenter's direction and scoring, and multiple sequel continuities.

  1. 1974Bob Clark's Black Christmas

    The holiday setting, threatening telephone calls, killer-eye photography, and unresolved ending help define the emerging slasher language.

  2. Mid-70sClark and Carpenter discuss a sequel

    Clark later recalled Carpenter asking whether he would make a sequel. Clark imagined Billy caught, institutionalized, escaping the next year, and returning to the house at Halloween. Clark later emphasized that Carpenter did not copy him.

  3. 1978John Carpenter's Halloween

    Carpenter directs, co-writes with Debra Hill, and scores the film. Michael Myers escapes confinement and returns to Haddonfield.

The Texas Chain Saw Massacre

Defining traits: Leatherface, a family dynamic, brutal true-story framing, and Tobe Hooper's documentary-like visual language.

  1. 1972Bryanston distributes Deep Throat

    Louis Peraino's company distributes - it does not produce - the inexpensive film. Its extraordinary returns demonstrate the commercial power of exploitation releases and create an appetite for another low-cost sensation.

  2. Backstory1969-73 - Hooper finds his nightmare

    After Eggshells, Austin filmmaker Tobe Hooper reunites with Kim Henkel, forms Vortex, raises roughly $140,000 from Texas investors, and shoots for six punishing weeks in extreme summer heat.

  3. 1973-74The business model moves into horror

    Hooper and Henkel have already built their film independently. Bryanston sees another low-cost exploitation opportunity and acquires worldwide distribution rights to the completed production.

  4. 1974The Texas Chain Saw Massacre

    Hooper directs and co-writes. The fictional story draws partly on Ed Gein and contemporary Texas violence; vérité photography and a “true story” frame make it feel frighteningly real.

  5. 1974-76Bryanston's deal turns bitter

    Bryanston pays $225,000 plus a promised 35% of profits for distribution rights, then fails to pay the production its full share. The company collapses in 1976 amid its Deep Throat obscenity case.

Accuracy notes

Bryanston distributed both Deep Throat and the completed Texas Chain Saw Massacre; it did not finance Hooper's production. The filmmaker branching from Friday the 13th Part III is Steve Miner; Sean S. Cunningham is the producer-director who mentored him.
